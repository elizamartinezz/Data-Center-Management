What does integrity in cybersecurity refer to?	Ensuring that data remains unchanged and accurate.

What is meant by availability in cybersecurity?	Ensuring timely and reliable access to information and services.

What does authorization ensure?	It confirms someone's permissions to access resources.

What is adversarial thinking in cybersecurity?	Understanding potential attacks to secure a system effectively.

What are common threats in information security?	Malware, privilege escalation, stolen credentials, and data tampering.

What is the significance of physical security in cybersecurity?	It involves managing risks in data centers, including pest control and environmental threats.

What are the five key objectives of cryptography?	Confidentiality, integrity, authenticity, non-repudiation, and access control.

What is the difference between a block cipher and a stream cipher?	A block cipher encrypts data block by block and requires padding, while a stream cipher encrypts data as it flows without padding.

What is a substitution cipher?	A method of encryption that converts one letter to another.

What is a transposition cipher?	A method of encryption that changes the position of letters in the text.

What is the only cipher that can achieve unconditional security?	One-time pad.

What is symmetric encryption?	A method where the same key is used for both encryption and decryption.

What is asymmetric encryption?	A method that uses mathematically related key pairs for encryption and decryption, involving public and private keys.

What is hybrid encryption?	A method that combines symmetric and asymmetric encryption, using asymmetric to distribute a symmetric key.

What is at-rest encryption?	Encryption of data when it is stored on a cloud server.

What is in-transit encryption?	Encryption of data while it is being transmitted to or from a cloud server.

What is the purpose of TLS in in-transit encryption?	To secure data during transmission by establishing a secure connection between client and server.

What is homomorphic encryption?	A method that allows computations on encrypted data without needing to decrypt it first.

What are the types of homomorphic encryption?	Partially homomorphic encryption (PHE), somewhat homomorphic encryption (SHE), and fully homomorphic encryption (FHE).

What is searchable encryption?	A method that allows searching through encrypted data without decrypting it first.

What is a zero-knowledge proof?	A method that allows a user to prove their identity without revealing their actual secret.

What is the Ali Baba Cave example used to illustrate?	It illustrates how a person can prove knowledge of a secret without revealing the secret itself.

What is the primary concern when sending data to the cloud?	Data breaches, insider threats, regulatory compliance, and the risk of outsourcing sensitive tasks.

What is the role of a key encryption key (KEK)?	It encrypts data encryption keys (DEKs) to secure access to encrypted data.

What is the significance of the TLS handshake?	It establishes a secure connection by negotiating encryption parameters between client and server.

What happens during the certificate verification step of TLS?	The client verifies the server's digital certificate with a Certificate Authority (CA).

What is the key distribution problem in symmetric encryption?	The challenge of securely sharing the encryption key with the parties involved.

What is a digital certificate?	An electronic document that uses a digital signature to bind a public key with an identity.

What is the purpose of a search token in searchable encryption?	To allow the cloud to match encrypted keywords without revealing the contents.

What is the main advantage of hybrid encryption?	It combines the efficiency of symmetric encryption with the secure key distribution of asymmetric encryption.

What does the term 'data at rest' refer to?	Data that is stored and not actively being transmitted or processed.

What is the main function of the public key in asymmetric encryption?	To encrypt data that can only be decrypted by the corresponding private key.

What is a side channel attack?	An attack that steals information from a victim by observing physical implementation rather than breaking theoretical properties.

What are common methods used in side channel attacks?	Timing attacks and power analysis.

What is instruction speculation?	When a CPU guesses ahead and starts executing instructions before knowing for sure they're needed to speed up processing.

What is a cache timing attack?	A method that manipulates cache to a known state, waits for victim activity, and examines changes to infer information.

What is the 'Flush and Reload' technique?	A cache timing attack where relevant addresses are flushed from cache, then reloaded and timed to see if the victim accessed them.

What is the Spectre vulnerability?	A vulnerability that allows an attacker to manipulate a CPU's branch predictor to access restricted information.

What are cross VM attacks?	Attacks where an attacker runs a VM on the same host as the victim to measure shared resources.

What is a covert channel attack?	A method to communicate information between two computer processes that are not allowed to communicate by hiding info in shared resources.

What are the two types of covert channels?	Storage channels and timing channels.

What is a storage channel in covert attacks?	Data transmitted by writing or abstaining from writing, such as writing into RAM.

What is a timing channel in covert attacks?	Data modulated into the timing or occurrence of events, such as the timing between network packets.

What is coresidency in cloud computing?	The situation where virtual machines from different customers are placed on the same physical host.

Why is coresidency studied?	Due to security risks from side-channel attacks and performance impacts from resource contention.

What is Amazon EC2?	A cloud computing service that allows users to create virtual servers with various configurations.

What are the user options for Amazon EC2?	Region, availability zone, and instance type.

What is the significance of availability zones in Amazon EC2?	Each availability zone has independent failure modes, providing redundancy and reliability.

What is cloud cartography?	Mapping cloud infrastructure to predict and facilitate co-residency with a target.

What is the purpose of domain mapping in cloud infrastructure?	To map internal and external domain names to IP addresses for better resource management.

What is a timing channel?	A method where information is hidden by triggering or delaying events at specific time intervals.

What is the role of TCP/IP headers in covert channels?	TCP/IP headers can serve as carriers for covert channels by hiding data in fields ignored during packet processing.

How can ICMP echo requests be used in covert channels?	They can tunnel arbitrary user data through the request/reply mechanism.

What is the bandwidth capacity of DNS for holding arbitrary text?	110-220 bytes per request.

What is the significance of timing in network channels?	Information can be hidden by varying the timing of packet transmissions.

What is a power analysis attack?	An attack that reads bits from powerline traces to infer secret information.

What is differential power analysis?	A method that looks for statistical differences in power traces based on guesses of a key bit.

What is a timing attack on software optimization?	An attack that leverages key-dependent timings of modular exponentiations in RSA.

What is the impact of speculative execution on security?	It can lead to vulnerabilities like Spectre, where incorrect execution can affect cache state.

What is the primary function of identity management?	To manage individual, entities, or group authentication, authorization, roles, and privileges across the system.

Why is centralized identity management beneficial?	It allows for tracking and managing identities across applications, uniform security settings, and cost control by reducing duplicated identity management.

What is activation in the context of identity management?	The creation of user identity, which is a one-time event that assigns privileges.

What does authentication refer to?	The process of verifying the identity of users, typically using credentials that include a public known part and one or more secrets.

What is multi-factor authentication?	A security scheme requiring two or more factors of something you know, something you have, and something you are.

What are the pros of password-based authentication?	It is simple, easy to implement, requires no special hardware, and is widely used across systems.

What are the cons of password-based authentication?	Weak passwords can be easily guessed, password reuse increases risk, phishing attacks can occur, databases can be leaked, and forgotten passwords lead to support overhead.

What is knowledge-based authentication?	An authentication method that verifies a user's identity based on personal information or answers to pre-set questions.

What is static knowledge-based authentication?	A method where users set answers to predefined questions during registration.

What is dynamic knowledge-based authentication?	A method using system-generated questions based on public or private data sources, without prior user setup.

What is the process of account recovery when a password is forgotten?	Initiate recovery, input associated communication channel (email), obtain a temporary link, and reset the password through that link.

What is the role of email addresses in account recovery?	Email addresses serve as a master key for all accounts and should remain private.

What are some authentication methods?	Password-based, two-factor/multi-factor authentication, biometric authentication, knowledge-based authentication, behavioral authentication, single sign-on, token-based, and certificate-based authentication.

What is the impact of phishing attacks on authentication?	Phishing can trick users into revealing their credentials, compromising their accounts.

What is the importance of cache monitoring in VM security?	It helps detect unauthorized access and potential co-residency threats by analyzing cache usage patterns.

What is multi-factor authentication (MFA)?	A security method requiring users to provide two or more types of credentials before gaining access to services.
