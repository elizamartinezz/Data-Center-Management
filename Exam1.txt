Q: Why isn't theory of computation enough to "make money"?	A: Because making money requires understanding user needs, business models, and current technology—not just theory.

Q: How do software engineers stay relevant?	A: Read research papers, follow tech news, try new products, and keep up with AI advancements.

Q: What is the key to monetizing software?	A: Identifying user needs, finding gaps, and applying the right business model.

Q: Why is understanding business models important for developers?	A: It determines how software generates revenue and scales.

Q: What is open-source software?	A: Software with publicly available source code that can be modified and redistributed.

Q: How do open-source projects make money?	A: Through reputation, support, consulting, and enterprise services.

Q: Examples of open-source software?	A: Linux, Android, Docker, Firefox.

Q: What is free-download (closed-source) software?	A: Free software with proprietary source code used to attract users.

Q: What is "free with purchase" software?	A: Software bundled with hardware purchases.

Q: What is free-with-advertisement software?	A: Software that generates revenue through ads.

Q: Examples of ad-based software?	A: Facebook, Google Search/Maps, YouTube.

Q: Risk of outdated licensed software?	A: Security vulnerabilities.

Q: What is a freemium model?	A: Free version with limitations and paid subscription for full access.

Q: What is a premium model?	A: Mandatory subscription after a trial period.

Q: Examples of premium software?	A: Adobe Creative Cloud, Microsoft Office 365.

Q: What does XaaS stand for?	A: Everything as a Service.

Q: How does XaaS pricing work?	A: Users pay only for what they use, often with a free tier.

Q: Examples of XaaS platforms?	A: iCloud, Dropbox, ChatGPT, Duo Security, Google Drive.

Q: What is IaaS?	A: Infrastructure as a Service—virtual machines, storage, networking.

Q: Example of IaaS?	A: AWS EC2.

Q: What is PaaS?	A: Platform as a Service—hosted environment for building apps.

Q: Examples of PaaS?	A: Google App Engine, cloud gaming platforms.

Q: What is SaaS?	A: Software delivered over the cloud.

Q: Examples of SaaS?	A: Google Drive, Microsoft 365.

Q: IaaS vs PaaS vs SaaS?	A: IaaS = Host, PaaS = Build, SaaS = Consume.

Q: Efficiency benefit of XaaS?	A: Faster, more reliable development.

Q: Cost benefit of XaaS?	A: No physical IT infrastructure and subscription-based pricing.

Q: Scalability benefit of XaaS?	A: Easily scale resources and experiment safely.

Q: ROI benefit of XaaS?	A: Access to high-quality services and increased returns.

Q: What causes cloud downtime?	A: Internet dependency and service outages.

Q: Performance risks in cloud computing?	A: Bandwidth limits, latency, storage bottlenecks.

Q: What is vendor lock-in?	A: Dependence on a single provider making migration difficult.

Q: Major security risks in cloud systems?	A: Data breaches, credential theft, isolation failures.

Q: What is a public cloud?	A: Cloud services shared by many users over the internet.

Q: Examples of public cloud providers?	A: AWS, Google Cloud.

Q: What is a private cloud?	A: Cloud infrastructure dedicated to one organization.

Q: Who uses private clouds?	A: Banks, healthcare orgs, large enterprises.

Q: What is a hybrid cloud?	A: Combination of on-premises and cloud infrastructure.

Q: Use cases for hybrid cloud?	A: Seasonal businesses, TurboTax, legacy systems.

Q: What is a community cloud?	A: Cloud shared by organizations with common compliance needs.

Q: Factors when choosing a cloud deployment model?	A: Security, cost, scalability, control, and performance.

Q: What is cloud migration?	A: Moving data or applications to the cloud or between clouds.

Q: What is rehosting?	A: Moving applications to the cloud without changes.

Q: What is replatforming?	A: Making small changes to optimize for the cloud.

Q: What is rebuilding?	A: Rewriting applications from scratch.

Q: What is replacing?	A: Retiring old apps and using cloud-native solutions.

Q: What is cloud computing?	A: On-demand access to shared computing resources over the internet with minimal management effort.

Q: What are the 5 NIST characteristics of cloud computing?	A: On-demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, measured service.

Q: What is resource pooling?	A: Cloud providers dynamically allocate shared resources to multiple customers based on demand.

Q: What is rapid elasticity?	A: Ability to quickly scale resources up or down as demand changes.

Q: What is measured service?	A: Users pay only for the resources they consume.
